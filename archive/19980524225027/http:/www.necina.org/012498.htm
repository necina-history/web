The role of SONET and Optical Networking on the future of Telecommunications
 

Abstract:

Today SONET is a more than a $4 billion dollar industry.  SONET was
first introduced in 1989 and has since grown and changed with the needs
of the network and marketplace.    Over that time period, over $15
billion of SONET/SDH equipment has been installed throughout the world.
All major carriers use SONET as a fundamental technology to manage
fiber and bandwidth.    

SONET provides physical layer functionality to a network.    It provides
reliable transmission of data over fiber.  It allows carriers to easily
allocate bandwidth for circuit or packet data.     It allows a carrier
to maximize and manage the use of an embedded fiber optic network.

Even though other technologies (ATM, Optical Networking) may appear to
replace SONET,  SONET will still remain an important building block for
large scale networks because of 3 unique features:    
1.  rich and easy to use OAM  features built into the protocol
    (operations, administration, and maintenance),
2.  high reliability and survivability with restoration times within
    milliseconds, 
3.  mature set of standards to allow interoperability.

Optical networking holds the key to massive growth in bandwidth.
Advances in Optical networking are allowing carriers to increase
bandwidth capacity by a factor of 16 without having to install
additional fiber.    Optical networking also promises to eliminate
traditional multiplexers with pure optical crossconnects and switches,
making equipment costs significantly lower.

Optical Networking is a new technology, but it should be viewed as an
extension and complement to  SONET.  Optical Networking's value to the
marketplace is similar to SONET's (fiber bandwidth management).   The
difference being Optical Networking is achieving it using wavelength
multiplexing  instead of TDM (time division multiplexing).   Optical
Networking success will depend on it's ability to provide similar
functionality SONET does today.   Right now, there are several key
obstacles that prevent Optical Networking from achieving universal
deployment.     

Another important trend in telecommunications is the growth of data and
packet based networks.   SONET and Optical Networking will play a
significant role because of it's unique bandwidth management and
restoration capability.    ATM and IP are technologies that will ride
over SONET.     How well ATM or  straight IP merge with SONET will
depend on the application and service requirements.      Also important
is designing a combination that allows each layer (SONET, ATM, IP) to
function in a standards compliant manner.  Some proposed implementations
cause nonstandard SONET behavior.   These types of implementations
might be fine for private enterprise networks but would not be
compatible with most carrier's embedded SONET networks.

Overall, the telecommunications industry is seeing great growth in
Packet Technologies (ATM, IP) and Optical Networking.   Both these
technologies will have to merge with a large embedded base of  SONET
networks.   Understanding the unique functions of SONET will help you
understand how Data and Optical Networking will be deployed in carrier
size networks.
