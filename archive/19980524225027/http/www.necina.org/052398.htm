IPSec, SSL/TLS and IKE -- Internet Security and Key Management 

Abstract:

In the past few years, Internet has re-discovered the importance of
security and in the process re-shaped the landscape of this area. The new
security protocols such as SSL, IPSec, ISAKMP+Oakley or IKE, and the new
security infrastructures such as PKIX, SPKI, RADIUS and Kerberos, can
confuse laymen and professionals alike who are eager to learn about these
technologies and appreciate their values.

In this last seminar of the year, our own SIG seminar director will try to
provide a birdview of this exciting but sometimes confusing field. First,
he will provide an conceptual framework about security requirements,
security protocols and security infrastructure. Then, he will use IPSec,
SSL and IKE as examples to explain how protocols and infrastructures work
together to satisfy the communication security requirements of the
Internet. Finally, he will mention a few new technologies appearing on the
horizon. Hopefully, the talk will offer you a high-level introduction to
the field and brief description of several emerging standards.

Presenter:

John Zao is NECINA '97 SIG seminar director. He is a senior scientist of
Network Security Dept., BBN Technologies, and a member of GTE
Internetworking Security Practice Center. Currently, John is the principal
investigator of a DARPA research projects on security policy management, a
key personnel of DARPA Information Assurance Program, and a member of US 
rmy Task Force XXI Tactical Internet Advisory Panel. John obtained his PhD 
in Computer Science from Harvard University in 1995. His technical
interests include mobile internet security, security policy management,
communication security architecture and distributed algorithms.
